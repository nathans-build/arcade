# ELA question bank: notes

This bank follows the North Carolina Standard Course of Study for English Language Arts. The State Board of Education adopted it in 2017, and schools began using it in 2018-19. It is still the course of study in place for 2026-27.

- There are 13 files (`gK.ts` to `g12.ts`) with 18 questions each, 234 in all.
- Every question is written with the correct answer first (`answer: 0`).
- All passages are original writing.
- Grades 9-10 use the `9-10` band codes (English I and II). Grades 11-12 use the `11-12` band codes (English III and IV).

## NC-specific differences we could confirm (from search-result excerpts of NC DPI documents)

- **Reading: Foundational Skills numbering in K-1 differs from the Common Core.** NC added a handwriting standard at RF.x.2, which moves the later standards up by one number:
  - RF.K.2 and RF.1.2: handwriting (print upper- and lowercase letters)
  - RF.K.3 and RF.1.3: phonological awareness (rhyming, syllables, and sounds at the start, middle and end of words)
  - RF.K.4 and RF.1.4: phonics and word analysis
  - RF.K.5 and RF.1.5: fluency
  - This bank uses the NC numbers. For example, rhyming is coded RF.K.3, not RF.K.2 as it would be in the Common Core.
- **Grade 2 RF.** Search excerpts show RF.2.4 as phonics and word analysis and RF.2.5 as fluency. Grade 2 phonics items are therefore coded RF.2.4.
- **Grades 3-5 RF** appear to keep the Common Core numbering (RF.3.4 is fluency), so RF.3.3 would be phonics. No grade 3-5 item is coded RF.
- **Language standards are written as a "continuum".**
  - L.x.1 reads: "Demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English grammar and usage ... within the grade band grammar continuum."
  - L.x.2 is the same for capitalization, punctuation and spelling ("conventions continuum").
  - NC lists no lettered sub-standards (L.7.1a and so on), so this bank uses whole codes only, such as L.7.1.
  - L.3 (knowledge of language) and L.4-L.6 (vocabulary) follow the Common Core anchors.
- **Handwriting.** NC calls for legible handwriting in manuscript and cursive in grade 3, and in cursive in grades 4-5. No items test this.

## Codes to verify against the official NC DPI "Understanding the ELA SCOS" documents

Direct access to dpi.nc.gov and files.nc.gov was blocked in this environment, so the codes below rest on Common Core numbering or on partial search excerpts:

- **RF.K.1** (print concepts): assumed unchanged.
- **RF.2.4**: confirmed only from an excerpt. The contents of RF.2.1 to RF.2.3 in NC are unconfirmed.
- **Grade K reading and language standards: RL.K.3, RL.K.6, L.K.1 (plurals), L.K.5.** One search excerpt paraphrased NC RL.K.1 differently from the Common Core. Check the kindergarten RL/RI wording and numbering.
- **RL.1.5 and RI.1.5 / RI.2.5** (text features): assumed to match the Common Core.
- **W codes (W.6.1, W.7.1, W.8.1, W.9-10.1, W.11-12.1) for argument writing.** NC reorganized some writing standards in 2017. W.8.2 (informative) and W.8.3 (narrative) are confirmed by excerpt. W.x.1 (argument) is assumed.
- **Grammar items coded L.x.1 and punctuation items coded L.x.2.** These are correct at the anchor level. The exact grade in which NC's continuum introduces each skill (for example, verb mood in grade 8 or relative pronouns in grade 4) follows Common Core placement.
- **All RL/RI numbers from grade 3 up.** These are assumed to match the Common Core anchors: 1 details, 2 theme/main idea, 3 characters, 4 word meaning/tone, 5 structure, 6 point of view, 8 argument, 9 source material.

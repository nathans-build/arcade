# Trail Ledger sources

Every event card and landmark in `src/data/*.ts` carries its own `source` string, and
`npm test` fails if one is missing. This file collects them, says what each one supports, and
records how the facts were checked.

**How facts were checked (October 2026).** The sandbox blocks most websites, so facts were
verified through WebSearch result excerpts, not full page reads. Treat the items under "Spot-check
before publishing" as likely but not certain.

**Primary sources and copyright.**
- Excerpts marked **public domain** quote works published before 1929:
  - Fries (1922)
  - Hastings (1845)
  - Scott, *Journal of Negro History* (1919)
- Excerpts and readings drawn from modern, copyrighted work are paraphrased and labeled
  **retold** in the game. These include Mia Bay's *Traveling Black* (2021), the Hecox memoir
  (published 1966), and the Augusta and Spangenberg lines (translation wording).

## Wagon Road South, 1753

| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| 15 Brethren left Bethlehem Oct 8, 1753, with 6 horses and one wagon; about 520 miles in about 40 days; arrived Nov 17, 1753; cut the last 2½ miles of road to Hans Wagner's cabin; 11 stayed | Historic Bethabara Park, "North Carolina, a New Home" (historicbethabara.org); City of Winston-Salem, "Moravian Story"; NCpedia, "Hans Wagner's cabin" |
| Lovefeast while "the wolves howled loudly, but all was well with us" (Bethabara Diary, Nov 17, 1753) | Fries, *Records of the Moravians in North Carolina*, vol. 1 (1922), as quoted by Historic Bethabara Park. **Public domain** |
| Route via Winchester and Staunton; hilly ground was hard on the horses; "the good road ended at Augusta" | Salem Academy and College, "The Route"; Walking Through Salem, "The Great Philadelphia Wagon Road". **Retold** |
| Great Wagon Road was the most important frontier road in the western Piedmont; it followed the Warriors' Path; it met the Catawba/Cherokee trading path at the Trading Ford on the Yadkin | NCpedia, "Great Wagon Road"; NCpedia, "Indian Trading Paths" |
| Spangenberg's 1752 survey party was stopped by six Cherokee hunters who became friendly | Search excerpt of the Spangenberg diary (Fries 1922). **Retold** |
| Catawba deerskin trade (cloth, kettles, knives, guns); deerskins second to rice among SC exports in the 1740s | South Carolina Encyclopedia, "Deerskin trade"; NCpedia, "Great Trading Path" |
| Gnadenhütten (1746), about 125 Lenape and Mohican people by 1753; Lenapehoking | Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia, "Moravians" and "Lenape People (Continuing Presence)"; Muhlenberg College |
| Walking Purchase of 1737 (disputed deed, staged walk, about 1.2 million acres) | Lehigh Valley Public Media, "The Walking Swindle" (search excerpt) |
| Susquehannock and Conestoga; land set aside early 1700s; Paxton Boys 1763 | Susquehanna National Heritage Area, "RiverRoots"; Historical Marker Database 121989; Wikipedia summary (search excerpt) |
| Moravians and slavery: Franke hired 1763; Sam purchased 1769 | Historic Bethabara Park, "The Stories of Bethabara's Enslaved" |
| Wachovia bought from Lord Granville | Common knowledge in NCpedia "Wachovia" entries. Spot-check |

**Distances.** Leg lengths are game distances that add up to the published 520 miles. The plan
said "about 455"; Historic Bethabara gives 520. Ferry fees and all store prices are game numbers.

## Westward Trail, 1846

| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| Hastings's food list: 200 lb flour or meal, 150 lb bacon, 10 lb coffee, 20 lb sugar, 10 lb salt per person | Lansford Hastings, *The Emigrants' Guide to Oregon and California* (1845), quoted via Oregon Pioneers, "Food on the Oregon Trail". **Public domain.** Wording spot-check |
| About 2,000 miles; Fort Laramie about 640 miles; about 15 miles a day; 4–6 months | Oregon Encyclopedia, "Oregon Trail"; BLM NHOTIC, "Wagons"; NPS, "Traveling the Emigrant Trails" |
| Fort Laramie (Fort John) run by the American Fur Company, finished 1841; Lakota buffalo-robe trade; Army purchase 1849 | WyoHistory.org, "Fort Laramie" and "Fort John" |
| Pappan's ferry (1842), run by brothers of Kanza (Kaw) and French descent | Historical Marker Database 65025; Legends of Kansas |
| Independence Rock, "Great Register of the Desert", the July 4 target | WyoHistory.org; NPS. The Hecox 1846 Fourth of July memory is **retold** |
| Sublette (Greenwood) Cutoff, 1844: saves about 85 miles and 7 days; about 45 waterless miles | WyoHistory.org, "Parting of the Ways"; NPS, "True Parting of the Ways" |
| Fort Hall: built 1834 by Wyeth, Hudson's Bay Company from 1837; Shoshone and Bannock homeland; Salmon Falls fishery and trade | Idaho State Historical Society Reference Series 121, 184 and 62 |
| Cayuse horse trading, and later vegetables, with emigrants | Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation, "Brief History of CTUIR" |
| Nez Perce traded clothing, food and horses on their own terms | University of Idaho Library, "Fur Trade Era" |
| Celilo Falls and The Dalles: Wasco and Wishram trading center; "great mart"; Wascopam Mission 1838 | Northwest Power and Conservation Council, "Celilo Falls"; Wikipedia summaries (search excerpts) |
| Barlow Road opened 1846; toll $5 a wagon and 10¢ a head of livestock; about 145–150 wagons the first year; Laurel Hill ropes and snubbing | Oregon Encyclopedia, "Barlow Road"; Historic Oregon City, "The Final Leg of the Trail"; Joel Palmer, *Journal of Travels* (1847) |
| War with Mexico declared May 13, 1846; Oregon Treaty June 1846, 49th parallel | Standard references. Spot-check |
| 1844 exclusion law (banned slavery and ordered Black settlers out); George Washington Bush settled north of the Columbia | Oregon Encyclopedia, "George Bush (c. 1790–1863)"; Historic Oregon City, "Exclusion Laws"; BlackPast, "The Black Laws of Oregon" |

**Prices.** Store prices and the trader rates on event cards are game numbers. The one sourced
price is the Barlow toll ($5.60 for a wagon and 6 oxen). The Kansas River ferry fee is a game
number.

## Northbound, 1917

| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| Parrish Street ("Black Wall Street"), NC Mutual founded 1898 by Merrick and Moore, praised by Washington and Du Bois | NCpedia, "Black Wall Street"; Durham County Library research guide |
| Chicago Defender's "Great Northern Drive", May 15, 1917; porters carried the paper south | Chicago Defender, "History of the Chicago Defender"; Driehaus Museum, "Pullman Porters" |

| Washington as the line where Jim Crow cars began southbound (so ended northbound); Jim Crow cars placed behind the engine | Mia Bay, "Jim Crow Journeys" (*Southern Spaces*, 2021). **Retold** |
| Wilson administration segregated federal offices (1913) | Standard references. Spot-check |
| Baltimore's 1910 ordinance (signed Dec 19, 1910), the first municipal residential segregation law | Baltimore Heritage, "1885–1929"; Johns Hopkins Urban Health appendix |

| Migrant letter: "I should have been here 20 years ago. I just begin to feel like a man… My children are going to the same school with the whites" | Emmett J. Scott, "Additional Letters of Negro Migrants of 1916–1918," *Journal of Negro History* 4 (Oct 1919), via search excerpt (teachtnhistory.org). **Public domain** |
| Silent Protest Parade, July 28, 1917: nearly 10,000 marchers, children first, women and children in white, muffled drums; followed East St. Louis | New-York Historical Society; Library of Congress research guide; Gilder Lehrman |
| 15th New York Infantry (369th) | Standard references. Spot-check |
| June 5, 1917 draft registration (men 21–30) | National Archives, "World War I Draft Registration Cards". Spot-check |
| Shoebox lunches; dining cars refusing or delaying service | NMAAHC, "Millions Move North"; Bay (retold) |

**Language.**
- The game never uses a slur, even inside quotations.
- Excerpts were chosen so that none contains one.
- "Negro" appears only in the titles of the 1919 journal articles, in source citations.

**Prices.** Rail fares, wages and meal prices are game numbers.

# Thread Chasers: world history detective chase (plan, not built)

Status (October 2026): planned. Decisions are in a GitHub issue. Grades **5–12 only**: K–4 see a
friendly "for grades 5 and up" screen.

## Why this mechanic

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Time-and-place detective chase** | **Chosen.** Each case follows one *thread*, the way one invention, good or idea moved across regions and centuries. You steer with **geography** (where next?), **chronology** (when?), **cause and effect** (why did it move?) and **sourcing** (which witness can you trust?). Answers are naturally 4 choices. It feels nothing like Trail Ledger's linear journey. |
| Trade caravan (Silk Road, trans-Saharan) | Runner-up. Too close to Trail Ledger's journey and supplies; it comes in as a small **Market stop** in v2. |
| Civilization-style settlement sim | Rejected: fictional history, pulls toward conquest, too big. |
| Archaeologist dig | Rejected: too close to Rock Driller. |

**Clue Compass:** the idea from `80s-90s-game-ideas.md` becomes K–5 present-day geography (community,
NC, US, world). Thread Chasers owns history for 5–12, and the two can share the map and clue engine.

**Stay clear of:**
- Carmen Sandiego: no red-coat thief, no ACME/V.I.L.E., no "gumshoe" ranks.
- *Time Riders in American History*.
- James Burke's *Connections*: don't use "Connections" in the name.

## Premise

Outside time stands the **Long Archive**, where the Great Loom weaves the story of how the world got
connected. **Knot**, a silly yarn-ball gremlin who is never evil, tugs threads loose and scatters
their *beads* (moments) across time and place. The arcade HERO (with an archivist's satchel) and
**Wick**, a lantern-moth guide whose lantern is the case clock, chase each thread bead by bead and
re-weave it.

**Title:**
- **Thread Chasers** is recommended (no game found by that name).
- History Menders is the alternative.
- Rejected: Threads of Time, Chrono Courier, Time Tangle and World Loom, which are all taken.

## Core loop (8–12 minutes per case)

1. **Case brief.** Wick reads the loose-thread card.
2. **Bead scene.** A pixel vignette with up to 3 **witnesses** and an artifact plaque. Each gives a
   clue to the *next* bead: geography, chronology, or economics/culture. Each interview costs 1 hour
   of lantern, so choosing whom to ask is a reading-strategy decision.
3. **Chrono-map jump.** 4 glowing pins (place + era), chosen with A–D, 1–4 or a tap. Distractors are
   the right place in the wrong century, the right century in the wrong place, or a plausible
   neighbor. Grades 9–12 use a **split dial**: pick the place, then the era.
4. **A wrong jump is a dead end.** A polite local explains *why it can't be here* ("Paper? Not here
   for another 400 years"). That explanation is the lesson. It costs 2 lantern hours, and the player
   goes back to the last bead. A single miss never ends the case.
5. **Source check** (grades 6–12). A primary-source excerpt with a sourcing or corroboration
   question. Debates are taught as debates: Talas and paper; how much Mansa Musa's gold affected
   Cairo.
6. **Re-weave finale.** Put the beads in order (repeated "what came next?" A–D questions, so the kit
   needs no new type), then answer "why did it move?" (trade, pilgrimage, war captives, migration,
   empire). Knot is caught.
7. **Lantern refuels:** an optional quick kit social question, +1 hour.
8. **Transmissions** between cases, as in the other games.
9. **Lantern runs out:** "the thread slips", restart from the last bead with points kept.

**Ranks:** Page → Archivist → Curator → Keeper of the Loom.

**Tone:** no combat, Knot is never hurt, and no empire "wins". Wars appear only as causes inside
clues.

## Grade adaptation

| | Grade 5 | 6–8 | 9–12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beads per case | 4 | 5 | 5–6 |
| Witnesses | 2 + plaque, every clue helps | 3 + plaque, one red herring | 3 + plaque, one unreliable or contradicting witness |
| Map | named pins, era labels | named pins, era on tap | split dial, unlabeled map |
| Clue length | ≤ 60 characters | ≤ 100 characters | ≤ 140 characters; excerpts about 80 words |
| Source checks | none | 1 per case | 2 per case |
| Re-weave | dates shown | centuries shown | dates hidden + a multi-causal "why" |
| Lantern | 14 hours | 12 hours | 10 hours |

**Questions:**
- The kit deck at the player's own grade for refuels and transmissions.
- The game's own case bank (clues, re-weave and "why" items as kit `Question` objects with NC codes).
- Everything is recorded with `recordAnswer`.

## Sensitive history

- **Principles:**
  - agency and multiple perspectives, with at least one non-European viewpoint per case
  - "reached", not "discovered"
  - numbers given as estimates (SlaveVoyages: about 12.5 million embarked, about 10.7 million
    survived the crossing)
  - colonialism shown with its harms and the resistance to it (e.g. Afonso I of Kongo's 1526
    letters)
  - slavery on every route, without whataboutism
- **Never playable:**
  - trading or transporting enslaved people (the 2015 "slave Tetris" backlash is the cautionary
    example)
  - conquest or picking a side in a war or genocide
  - any Holocaust or genocide stop in the chase
  - hate imagery
  - caricature
  - Knot on any sensitive screen
  - gore
- **Holocaust education** (NC's Gizella Abramson Act requires it in grades 6–12; USHMM warns against
  simulations):
  - v1 states at the UDHR bead that it followed World War II and the Holocaust.
  - v2 adds an **Archive of Remembrance** reading room for grades 7+: no timer, no points, no Knot, no
    music, plus USHMM and NC Council links. Build it only after educator review.
- **Review:**
  - every bead cites ≥ 2 sources (`docs/sources.md`)
  - a content lint in `npm test` (banned words; no Knot or timer on sensitive beads)
  - a social studies teacher reviews it, ideally with regional reviewers
  - Nathan and a parent playtest it
  - a "report a fact" note in the README

  - 6.H.1, 6.G.1.2, 6.E.1.1, 6.E.1.2, 6.B.1.1
  - 7.H.1.1, 7.G.1.2, 7.E.1.2, 7.C&G.1.1
  - WH.H.1, WH.H.1.2, WH.G.1.2, WH.E.1.2, WH.C&G.1
  - 5.G.1.2 and 5.G.1.3
- **Primary sources:**
  - Quoted only if public domain: Marco Polo (Yule), Ibn Battuta (Gibb 1929, verify PD), Equiano,
    Locke, Magna Carta, the 1789 Declaration, the UDHR, Analects (Legge).
  - Retold: al-Umari, al-Bakri, *Liber Abaci*.
- **Report:**
  - results by standard (Chronology, Diffusion, Trade networks, Sourcing…)
  - a "Loom" picture of the re-woven threads
  - practice next
  - misses with their dead-end explanations

## Look, scope, tests

- **Screens and characters:**
  - A 288×120 world map with rivers and blinking pins, and zoomed insets for the Mediterranean,
    Arabia and the Indian Ocean.
  - A timeline bar.
  - Bead scenes from about 10 tile sets.
  - Witnesses with respectful dress and natural skin tones; Wick and Knot.
  - Clue text sits below the canvas.
- **Controls:** arrows pick witnesses, Enter talks; answers on 1–4 / A–D or a tap.
- **Size:** similar to Page Quest (about 5,000 lines), and **content-heavy**: about 60% of the effort
  goes to writing and fact-checking. v1 is about 38 beads, about 340 clue lines, about 110 dead-end
  replies, about 12 excerpts and about 40 re-weave items.
- **Tests:**
  - beads in date order
  - no distractor pin that is accidentally correct
  - every dead end explained
  - clue lengths per band
  - valid codes
  - pins on land (or coast)
  - every case solvable with at least 3 mistakes
  - the content lint
  - a playtest at grades 3 (gate screen), 5, 7 and 11
- **Two-player** (v2): "Twin Archivists" pass-and-play co-op on a shared lantern, each player at
  their own grade.

## Open decisions (recommendations in bold)

1. Make Clue Compass K–5 present-day geography and share the engine: **yes**.
2. Kit deck for grades 10–12: **the player's own grade** for refuels and transmissions; the World
   History bank inside cases.
3. Archive of Remembrance: **v2, after educator review**, grades 7+, unscored.
4. Grade 5: **light cases only**, tagged 5.G.1.2/5.G.1.3.
5. Title: **Thread Chasers**, after USPTO and domain checks.
6. Keep Knot as the antagonist: **yes**, banned from sensitive beads.
7. Kit `sequence` question type: **not needed for v1**.
